# Health risks the TruDoc Gold test menu can flag

Source: `TruDoc_Gold_Test_Menu.xlsx` (core + annual panels). Each line is a distinct clinical risk or abnormality that a result outside its reference range can flag for a doctor to review. These are **flags, not diagnoses**: many need a repeat test or a reflex test to confirm (see the menu's Reflex Rules sheet).

**Needs TruDoc medical director sign-off before the number is used publicly.**

## Heart
1. High LDL cholesterol (hypercholesterolaemia): LDL, non-HDL, total cholesterol
2. Possible familial hypercholesterolaemia: LDL ≥190 mg/dL
3. High Lipoprotein(a), inherited heart risk: Lp(a)
4. High atherogenic particle count: ApoB
5. High triglycerides (hypertriglyceridaemia): triglycerides, VLDL
6. Very high triglycerides (pancreatitis risk): triglycerides ≥500 mg/dL
7. Low HDL cholesterol: HDL, total cholesterol/HDL ratio
8. High residual risk from remnant cholesterol: remnant cholesterol
9. Raised 10-year heart attack and stroke risk: PREVENT score

## Diabetes and metabolism
10. Prediabetes: HbA1c, fasting glucose
11. Type 2 diabetes: HbA1c, fasting glucose, eAG
12. Insulin resistance: HOMA-IR, TyG index, triglyceride/HDL ratio
13. High fasting insulin (hyperinsulinaemia): fasting insulin
14. Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia): fasting glucose

## Blood
15. Iron-deficiency anaemia: haemoglobin, ferritin, TSAT, MCV
16. Iron deficiency without anaemia: ferritin, TSAT, serum iron
17. Microcytic anaemia / possible thalassaemia trait: MCV, MCH, RBC count
18. Macrocytic anaemia: MCV, haemoglobin
19. Anaemia of chronic disease pattern: haemoglobin, ferritin, TIBC
20. High red cell mass (polycythaemia): haemoglobin, haematocrit, RBC count
21. Possible iron overload (haemochromatosis): ferritin, TSAT
22. Low platelets (thrombocytopenia): platelet count
23. High platelets (thrombocytosis): platelet count
24. Abnormal red cell size variation (anisocytosis): RDW-CV, RDW-SD
25. Abnormal platelet size: MPV, PDW
26. Possible red cell breakdown (haemolysis): indirect bilirubin, haemoglobin, urobilinogen

## Immunity and inflammation
27. Low white cell count (leukopenia): WBC
28. High white cell count (leukocytosis): WBC
29. Low neutrophils (neutropenia): neutrophils
30. High neutrophils, possible bacterial infection (neutrophilia): neutrophils
31. Low lymphocytes (lymphopenia): lymphocytes
32. High lymphocytes, possible viral infection (lymphocytosis): lymphocytes
33. High monocytes (monocytosis): monocytes
34. High eosinophils, allergy or parasites (eosinophilia): eosinophils
35. High basophils (basophilia): basophils
36. Immature white cells in the blood (left shift): immature granulocytes
37. Low-grade systemic inflammation: hs-CRP
38. Acute inflammation or infection: hs-CRP >10 mg/L
39. Raised inflammation ratios: NLR, PLR

## Liver
40. Fatty liver disease (MASLD) risk: ALT, AST, GGT, triglycerides
41. Advanced liver fibrosis risk: FIB-4
42. Liver cell injury: ALT, AST
43. Alcohol-related liver injury pattern: GGT, AST/ALT ratio
44. Blocked bile flow (cholestasis): ALP, GGT, direct bilirubin
45. Possible Gilbert syndrome: indirect bilirubin
46. High bilirubin (hyperbilirubinaemia): total bilirubin
47. Low albumin, poor nutrition or liver function (hypoalbuminaemia): albumin, total protein
48. High globulins (chronic inflammation or immune activity): globulin, A/G ratio
49. Low total protein: total protein

## Kidney
50. Chronic kidney disease (reduced filtration): eGFR, creatinine
51. Early kidney damage (albuminuria): uACR, urine albumin
52. Dehydration pattern: BUN/creatinine ratio, urine specific gravity
53. Sudden kidney function drop (acute kidney injury flag): creatinine, BUN
54. High uric acid (hyperuricaemia) / gout risk: uric acid

## Electrolytes and acid-base
55. Low sodium (hyponatraemia): sodium
56. High sodium (hypernatraemia): sodium
57. Low potassium (hypokalaemia): potassium
58. High potassium (hyperkalaemia): potassium
59. Low chloride (hypochloraemia): chloride
60. High chloride (hyperchloraemia): chloride
61. Metabolic acidosis: bicarbonate, anion gap
62. Metabolic alkalosis: bicarbonate

## Bone and minerals
63. High calcium (hypercalcaemia): calcium, corrected calcium
64. Low calcium (hypocalcaemia): calcium, corrected calcium
65. Low magnesium (hypomagnesaemia): magnesium
66. High magnesium (hypermagnesaemia): magnesium
67. Low phosphate (hypophosphataemia): phosphorus
68. High phosphate (hyperphosphataemia): phosphorus
69. Possible bone turnover disorder: ALP with normal GGT

## Thyroid
70. Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid): TSH, Free T4
71. Subclinical hypothyroidism: TSH, Free T4
72. Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid): TSH, Free T4
73. Subclinical hyperthyroidism: TSH, Free T4

## Hormones (men)
74. Low testosterone (male hypogonadism): total, free and bioavailable testosterone
75. Abnormal SHBG (affects usable testosterone): SHBG

## Prostate (men 50+)
76. Raised PSA, prostate cancer screening flag: PSA

## Vitamins
77. Vitamin D deficiency: vitamin D
78. Vitamin D insufficiency: vitamin D
79. Vitamin B12 deficiency: vitamin B12
80. Folate deficiency: folate

## Urinary
81. Urinary tract infection: nitrite, leukocyte esterase, urine WBC, bacteria
82. Blood in urine (haematuria): occult blood, urine RBC
83. Protein in urine (proteinuria): urine protein
84. Sugar in urine (glycosuria): urine glucose
85. Ketones in urine (ketonuria): urine ketones
86. Kidney stone risk: urine crystals, urine pH, uric acid
87. Bilirubin in urine (bilirubinuria): urine bilirubin
88. Abnormal urobilinogen: urobilinogen
89. Kidney-origin urine sediment: urine casts
90. Fungal urinary infection: yeast

## Biological age
91. Faster biological ageing: biological age (PhenoAge), age gap

**Total: 91 distinct health risks.**
